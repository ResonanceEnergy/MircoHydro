## 1. Ram pump — the competitive picture moved

**The "smart ram is an open niche" claim from our resonance research is now PARTIALLY REFUTED — and the correction matters for IP.**

- **HydroHammer Ltd (UK)** sells a composite-body "Smart Hydram" and a retrofit electronic controller (HARP) — automated start/stop/throttling. Pending patent [CN116802400A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN116802400A/en) (priority Dec 2020) claims stepper-driven variable valve aperture, magnetic valve-position sensing (self-powering), pressure sensors, accelerometer, electromagnetic valve-opening assist, wireless remote. [hydrohammer.co.uk](https://www.hydrohammer.co.uk/technology) **[V/P]**
- **What is STILL open** (verified by search, zero products/papers): **per-cycle closure-timing control** (our Young's zero-recoil phase-lock — HydroHammer throttles aperture, it does not phase-lock the cycle to 2L/a), **multi-ram phase coordination** (anti-synchronized banks smoothing headstock inflow — nothing published), **generation-optimized control** (tuning for turbine demand vs water delivery), and **motor-boosted hybrid rams** (nothing anywhere). → IP disclosure #2 must be REFRAMED: not "electronically controlled ram" (blocked) but "phase-locked zero-recoil control + bank coordination for generation" (open).
- **Our exact architecture has expired prior art**: IWHR's [CN107228042B](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107228042B/en) (2017, lapsed 2024) — waves→rams→30–50 m elevated tank→turbine→generator, with a rams-per-output formula. Blocks broad claims on the base architecture; **confirms freedom-to-operate**. Novelty must sit in control, coordination, and system optimization — exactly where our open niches are.
- **Water Powered Technologies (UK) "Venturo"** has marketed ram→storage→turbine generation since ~2018 at a claimed 85% **[V]** — still pre-commercial after 8 years. Validation of the concept; caution on the timeline.
- **Third-party proof our headstock-buffer is right**: Roberts et al. 2019 (*Renewable Energy*, Bournemouth/WPT) measured **direct** hammer→piston-crank conversion at 25.7% peak but **0.3–1.7% time-averaged** ([OA PDF](http://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/30975/1/renewable-1-BT.pdf)) **[M]**. Direct conversion is terrible; buffering through an elevated tank is the defensible route. Elgammi & Hamad 2022 ([*Renewable Energy*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2022.05.131)) drive a micro Pelton from hammer surges directly — closest academic system to ours, no headstock. **Nobody anywhere has published instrumented full-chain ram→headstock→penstock→Pelton efficiency. That dataset is open ground and we are building the tool that produces it.**
- **Scaling data for the bank strategy**: Kesharwani et al. 2021 ([PIMechE A](https://doi.org/10.1177/0957650921997202)) **[M]** field-tested large rams at **53–62% efficiency with a 40–50 L/s per-unit ceiling** — brackets our sim's ~18 L/s @ 300 mm finding from above and confirms banks (not one giant ram) are how you scale.

## 2. Nozzles & jets — fixed beats fancy at our head, and our CoV metric looks novel

- **The market leader ships the dumbest possible nozzle.** PowerSpout: fixed polymer tapered jets, 2–25 mm, literally cut to size with a knife, ball-valve isolation per jet, no spear valves anywhere in the line **[V]**. Hartvigsen sells machined fixed nozzles in 1/64" steps and publishes the rule **supply line ≥ 3× nozzle diameter** — adopt for our twin-jet manifold.
- **Why fixed wins at 4–15 m**: needle/spear internals cost ~0.8% in jet-profile non-uniformity plus wake-driven secondary flow (Benzon/Židonis, Lancaster **[M]**); a worn needle costs **~10% at part load** (Energies 2025 **[M]**). No needle → no wake, no erosion-critical part. If we ever need variable: modern steep angles (e.g., 50°/90° class, Khan/Staubli 2025 **[M]**), not textbook shallow ones.
- **The best-documented jet-quality lever is boring: a straight approach run.** Upstream bends measurably deflect/distort the jet; poor jet quality costs >2 efficiency points (Staubli line of work; TU Wien 2022 **[M]** — streamlined NACA support ribs cut secondary flow 40–50%). Keep the last bend several diameters upstream; our headstock→penstock→manifold geometry should be designed around this rule.
- **Our jet CoV metric appears to be novel**: the field uses dispersion angle, surface roughness, core deviation — **nobody uses coefficient-of-variation as the jet KPI** (verified by search across the Pelton jet literature). Publication/IP-relevant framing; LDV methods (Physics of Fluids 2023 **[M]**) are how we'd validate it physically.
- **Open niches verified empty**: honeycomb/straightener immediately before a micro nozzle (measured nowhere — cheap A/B arm, potential IP); additively-manufactured injector with measured turbine efficiency (nothing); **servo/stepper spear valve at pico scale (no product exists)** — pairs naturally with our self-tuning electronics if we ever want it.
- **Vortex nozzle (founder concept, D-3 A/B slot): the literature is against it.** Swirl increases jet spreading and centerline-velocity decay (NASA CR-180895 **[M]**); fire-industry "vortex" nozzles use swirl deliberately to *disperse*; the entire Pelton literature treats swirl as the defect to remove. **No test of a deliberately swirled impulse jet exists** — so the A/B is genuinely novel either way, but the expected result is a CoV penalty. Per the no-silent-substitution rule: the A/B stays (D-3 unchanged); this evidence goes in front of the founder, and the rig should be instrumented to measure the penalty magnitude — unpublished data whichever way it falls.
- Erosion at our head is a non-issue: 4–15 m → 9–17 m/s jets, ~3 orders of magnitude below high-head erosion rates. Brass/stainless/polymer all adequate; WC inserts are over-engineering unless silt load is extreme **[M]**.
- 3D printing: print polymer (SLA, smooth) nozzle iterations for CoV A/B testing; as-built metal AM internal channels are Ra 6–20 µm vs ~0.8 µm machined and measurably *hurt* jet coherence unless internally polished **[M]**.

## 3. Penstock & drive pipe — strong evidence arriving on a founder design element

- **Penstock material verdict: PE100-RC / PE4710 coiled HDPE is the 2026 state of the art at our diameters.** PPI fatigue data **[M]**: PE4710 survived **4.2 million surge cycles at 1.5× pressure class with zero failures**; 1980s projection >670 years vs 14–66 for rigid PVC; 100-yr design-life substantiated. PE's low wave speed (~200–330 m/s) also cuts surge amplitude ~4× — the penstock side is self-protecting (the OPPOSITE requirement from the drive pipe, where we need high wave speed — the two-materials logic in our architecture is now fully literature-backed). PE100-RC variant tolerates rocky native backfill = 1-day install in stony ground. PVC-O is the gasketed runner-up; GRP and aramid-reinforced pipe have no products at 50–150 mm — dead ends. Plain PVC near any hammer source is a fatigue liability (measured threshold: 0.66× rated at ≤20 °C, worse cold) — consistent with our COTS-model finding.
- **Fluted penstock (founder element X-canon, bench A/B roster): the measured evidence is against it, quantitatively.** (a) mm-scale internal helical ribs/flutes measured at **1.6–2.7× smooth-pipe friction** (Energies 2019 **[M]**; the entire heat-exchanger literature uses internal spirals *to increase* turbulence). (b) Riblets DO reduce drag in water pipes — but only at **~100–200 µm** groove scale at our Re, gains cap at ~6–11%, and every tested design **flips to a drag increase** when flow moves off the design point (ACS ES&T Water 2025 **[M]** — 20 mm pipe, exactly our physics). (c) No evidence anywhere that helical geometry upstream of a nozzle improves jet quality; Pelton practice is anti-swirl. **Per the no-silent-substitution rule: fluting is NOT removed** — this evidence goes to the founder as a decision item, and if the A/B proceeds, instrument to measure the predicted loss precisely. If the founder intent behind fluting is flow *conditioning*, the literature-supported equivalent is a honeycomb straightener at the manifold (§2) — worth presenting as the alternative that achieves the intent.
- **Surge control: compressorless bladder vessels are mainstream** — pre-charged bladder/diaphragm surge tanks, no compressor, no snifter-equivalent maintenance **[V]**. **Directly relevant to REQ-S1 (CSA B51)**: a commodity pre-charged vessel with existing CRN (Canadian Registration Number) may replace our engineered air chamber entirely — buying compliance instead of building it. Action: add CRN'd bladder-vessel option to the chamber-sizing study. Also: WPT's gas-spring accumulator patent EP2722575B1 shows the snifter-free direction is patented ground — the commodity CRN vessel route neatly sidesteps it.
- **Our headstock IS an open surge tank** — it hydraulically decouples ram-side hammer from the penstock. Third-party framing that the founder architecture embeds its own transient protection. Downstream, Pelton practice is **deflector-first shutdown** (dump the jet, close the valve over minutes — penstock never sees a fast closure) — adopt as the standard shutdown sequence; the deflector doubles as governor-less overspeed control.
- **Cold climate (−40 °C)**: Alberta frost depth is 2.4–3 m — full-depth burial is unrealistic; the northern practice is **shallow burial + factory pre-insulated pipe** ([GF Urecon](https://www.gfps.com/en-ca/products-solutions/brands/urecon.html) — Canadian, HDPE core, PU jacket, heat-trace channel) **+ keep water flowing + drain-on-shutdown** (PowerSpout cold-weather practice: pressure/wattage dip is the early freeze warning — free telemetry alarm for us). Frazil ice at intakes is measured science ([ASCE 2014/2023](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/abs/10.1061/(ASCE)CR.1943-5495.0000073) **[M]**): deep, slow-approach, coarse-opening intakes beat fine screens in supercooled water — REQ-P5 input.
- **1-day install enabler**: [Victaulic Style 905](https://www.victaulic.com/products/style-905-coupling-plain-end-hdpe-pipe/) mechanical couplings bite plain-end HDPE — **no fusion machine, no power, no temperature restriction, minutes per joint** (fusion below −20 °C is procedure-restricted). Push-fit compression (Philmac/Plasson) at ≤63 mm. Grooved rigid couplings for the steel drive pipe (~1/10 welding time, axial restraint under hammer).
- **Drive pipe: the boring answer survived.** Plain heavy-wall steel (sch 40/80, galvanized or stainless) is effectively infinite-life at our hoop stresses — corrosion, not fatigue, is the life limiter. Corrugated stainless is counter-indicated (stress concentrators, documented cycling failures); glass-lined is a dead end. Composite overwrap exists as corrosion armor **[M]** but no off-the-shelf product.

## 4. Turbine, generator, electronics — the ceiling is high and the cost floor is low

- **Turgo wins at our head, with a measured number that resets Machine B's ceiling**: Bristol (Williamson/Stark/Booker) measured **91% jet-to-mechanical at 3.5 m head, 87% at 1.0 m** on a low-head pico Turgo **[M]**. At 4–15 m the wheel is NOT our loss center — the ram and generator are. Supports and sharpens D-1 (Turgo 4–8 m band). No credible research shows Pelton beating Turgo below ~15 m at pico scale.
- **Machine A's 48.5% baseline is very beatable, and the recipe is local**: Adhikari & Wood (**University of Calgary**), [Energies 2018 open access](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/11/2/267) **[M/CFD]** — measured crossflows at 88% (Desai) and 90% (Totapally); their CFD took a 7 kW unit **69% → 91% by nozzle redesign alone**. Design rules: convert essentially all head in the nozzle, entry arc ≈ 90°, β₁ ≈ 39°, 30–35 blades. **Negative finding that saves money: the Ossberger-style internal guide vane REDUCED efficiency 85% → 77%** (Processes 2024 **[M]**) — regulate flow another way. Action: Machine A solver + FreeCAD runner macro get an Adhikari-recipe pass; realistic target moves from 48.5% toward measured high-70s (claim only what the solver + sim support).
- **Generators**: purpose-made PMGs are now cheaper than alternator conversions — [Motenergy](https://www.motenergy.com/hydro-power-products.html) sells hydro PMGs at **$179** (500 W–4 kW classes, impulse-native 1,200–2,400 rpm). Skip automotive alternators entirely (50–60% η, wrong rpm — measured dead end). Chinese axial-flux coreless units (>85% claimed, cogging <0.1 N·m — attractive for pulsed ram startup) publish **no partial-load curves** — dyno-validate before adopting **[V]**. PM partial-load efficiency stays high (copper loss ∝ I²) — optimize for annual duty cycle, not nameplate **[M]**.
- **Power electronics landscape**: MidNite Classic remains the only NA controller with an explicit hydro mode; **PowerSpout now field-validates a $471 SRNE 250 V MPPT for hydro** — a cheap OEM-bundle candidate. Victron works with settings care and gives **free VRM cloud telemetry via Cerbo GX** — the zero-engineering path to our ship-with-telemetry commitment (REQ-P4). Grid-tie: no inverter anywhere is UL1741-listed *for hydro as a source* — practice is presenting the turbine as a PV-like 150–400 V DC source into a listed PV string inverter (Solis formally approves the PowerSpout pairing), AHJ case-by-case; or DC-couple through a listed hybrid inverter. REQ-S2 language should reflect this honestly.
- **Three verified white spaces we could own**: hydro-specific MPPT (none emerged in a decade), UL1741 hydro-source listing (nobody has one), micro-hydro fleet telemetry product (nothing exists; our <$100 ESP32/LTE-M BOM or VRM freeride both work).
- **The number that disciplines our BOM**: a complete validated pico powertrain — Motenergy PMG $179 + Turgo wheel $95 + SRNE MPPT $471 — is **under $750**. That is the cost floor Machine B's powertrain must beat or absorb; TWO_MODEL_COMPARISON BOMs get re-checked against it.
- 3D-printed runners: LPBF hybrid pico Pelton beat a commercial baseline by 8.2–32.7% (rotational-speed metric) mainly by fixing blunt cast splitters **[M]**; ORNL runs a $15M AM-for-hydro program. AM's win is geometry fidelity and iteration speed, not peak η — print polymer for dev, mold/cast for production (Hartvigsen's $120 glass-nylon vs $408 cast stainless spoons frame our make/buy).
- Untested-but-promising cheap experiment: direct-drive e-bike hub motor (~$100–200) coupled to a *fast* Turgo — published failures are all rpm mismatch on slow waterwheels; at impulse rpm the kV match is plausible and **nobody has published the efficiency map**.
