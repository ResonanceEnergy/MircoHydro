# Evidence Record — Water Memory / Structured Water

**Standard:** Court-record. Both sides catalogued in full. Every item graded. No verdict.
**Grading key:**
- **[M]** — Measured / documented. A real, recorded event or peer-reviewed measurement (this includes a *failed replication*, which is a documented event even when what it disproves is contested).
- **[I]** — Interpreted / contested. The datum may be real; the meaning assigned to it is disputed.
- **[L]** — Unreplicated / anecdotal / advocacy. Single-source, never independently reproduced, or an opinion rather than a measurement.

A grade attaches to a *claim*, not to a *person*. A failed replication is [M] as an event; the inference "therefore memory of water is impossible" is [I].

## PART A — SYMPATHETIC RECORD

### A1. Benveniste 1988 — the origin paper [M as publication; I as effect]
- **Citation:** Davenas E, Beauvais F, Amara J, Oberbaum M, Robinzon B, Miadonna A, Tedeschi A, Pomeranz B, Fortner P, Belon P, Sainte-Laudy J, Poitevin B, Benveniste J. "Human basophil degranulation triggered by very dilute antiserum against IgE." *Nature* **333**(6176):816–818, 30 June 1988.
- **What was published [M]:** Human basophils degranulated when exposed to anti-IgE antiserum diluted in series (1:10 steps) to concentrations as extreme as ~10⁻¹²⁰ — i.e. past the Avogadro limit, where statistically **no molecule of the original antibody remains**. Degranulation did not fall monotonically to zero; the authors reported it **recurred in peaks** at successive high dilutions, and only if the water was **vigorously shaken (succussed)** between steps.
- **Co-reporting labs [M]:** The result was presented as reproduced across **four laboratories in four countries** — France (INSERM U200, Benveniste's lab, Clamart), Israel (Hebrew University, Jerusalem), Italy (Milan), and Canada (Toronto). Co-authors span those sites.
- **Publisher's own caveat [M]:** *Nature* took the unusual step of appending an editorial "reservation" and publishing only on condition that Benveniste admit independent investigators to witness a replication. This is the co-reported fact that later became the central dispute.

### A2. Later Benveniste — "digital biology" [L]
- After 1988 Benveniste claimed molecular activity could be **recorded electronically** (as an audio-frequency EM signal), **transmitted/replayed** into naïve water, and reproduce the biological effect — "digital biology." He founded **DigiBio** (Paris).
- **Status [L]:** A **blinded, independent test** organized with US skeptics and coordinated statistician Jay Rosen (and involving Randi's foundation), reported in 2004–2006 contexts, and an earlier team led by physicist Wayne Jonas / Cyril Smith arrangements, **did not reproduce** the transmission effect under blinding. No independent lab confirmed digital biology. Grade [L] — unreplicated.

### A3. Montagnier 2009–2014 — EM signals from DNA dilutions [M as publication; L as effect]
- **Citations [M]:** Montagnier L, Aïssa J, Ferris S, Montagnier J-L, Lavallée C. "Electromagnetic signals are produced by aqueous nanostructures derived from bacterial DNA sequences." *Interdisciplinary Sciences: Computational Life Sciences* **1**(2):81–90 (2009). Companion paper same journal/year reported EM signals from HIV DNA in treated patients' plasma at dilutions 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁸.
- **Claim [L]:** Highly diluted, succussed solutions of certain DNA sequences emit low-frequency (~7 Hz range) electromagnetic signals; the signal is destroyed by heating/freezing and requires prior dilution + agitation. A 2010–2011 preprint ("DNA waves and water," arXiv, Montagnier et al.) claimed a DNA sequence could be **"reconstituted" by PCR in a tube that had only been exposed to the EM signal** of the original — popularly, "DNA teleportation."
- **Process objections on record [M]:** The 2009 flagship paper was **submitted 3 Jan 2009, revised 5 Jan, accepted 6 Jan** — a three-day review — in a journal on whose editorial board Montagnier sat (later chairman). Critics (e.g. Philip Ball) called it "utterly implausible."
- **Replication status [M]:** **No independent replication** exists in the literature, for or against, by a neutral group. Montagnier relocated relevant work to Jiaotong University, Shanghai (~2010).
- **Retraction status [M]:** The papers have **never been formally retracted.**
- **DARPA / military interest [L — flagged unverified]:** Popular accounts assert defense/DARPA interest in the "signal transmission" idea. **The record here does not contain a documented DARPA grant or contract** tied to Montagnier's DNA-EM work. Treat any "the military was interested" claim as **unsourced** until a grant number or contract surfaces. (This is exactly the kind of claim a court record flags, not repeats.)

### A4. Gerald Pollack — Exclusion Zone (EZ) / "fourth phase" water [M for the phenomenon; I for the interpretation]
- **Lab:** Gerald Pollack, University of Washington (Bioengineering); a funded, publishing lab. Book: *The Fourth Phase of Water* (2013).
- **What IS measured [M]:** Adjacent to a hydrophilic surface (classically **Nafion**, a sulfonated polymer), an "exclusion zone" forms in which suspended solutes and microspheres are **pushed away**, out to tens–hundreds of micrometres. In this zone the lab reports, with instruments:
  - **Solute/particle exclusion** (directly imaged).
  - **Charge separation** — the EZ measures **net negative**, the bulk beyond it net positive, a measurable potential difference.
  - **A distinct UV absorbance peak near ~270 nm.**
  - **Light dependence** — incident light, especially **infrared**, *expands* the EZ, i.e. adds energy that grows the zone.
  These are peer-reviewed, reproducible bench observations (e.g. Zheng, Chai, Yoo & Pollack et al.).
- **What is CONTESTED [I]:** Whether these observations require a **new, long-range ordered "fourth phase"** of water (Pollack's interpretation), or are explained by **known interfacial chemistry** — ion-exchange from Nafion, diffusiophoresis/electro-diffusion, colloidal double layers, and pH gradients. A detailed critical review (Elton, Spencer, Legge et al., "Exclusion Zone Phenomena in Water — A Critical Review," 2020; arXiv:1909.06822) **accepts the EZ is real** but argues the magnitude and the "fourth phase / long-range structuring" reading are not supported and are better explained conventionally. The split is precise: **phenomenon [M], mechanism [I].**

### A5. Del Giudice / Preparata — QED coherence-domain theory [I / L]
- **What it is:** Emilio Del Giudice (1940–2014), Giuliano Preparata, and Giuseppe Vitiello developed a **quantum-electrodynamics (QED)** theory in which liquid water forms **coherence domains** (~0.1 µm) whose molecules oscillate in phase with a trapped EM field (foundational paper: Del Giudice, Preparata, Vitiello, "Water as a free electric dipole laser," *Physical Review Letters* **61**:1085, 1988).
- **Status in the field [I → L]:** It is **published theoretical physics** in reputable venues, but it is **not accepted by mainstream condensed-matter physics** and has **no confirming experimental test** that the wider community recognizes. It functions as the main *theoretical scaffold* offered for water memory, so its own unconfirmed status matters. Grade: a real theory [published], **experimentally unconfirmed [L]**, interpretively contested [I].

### A6. Homeopathy clinical meta-analyses — both directions [M as studies; I as inference]
- **Linde 1997 (sympathetic-leaning result) [M]:** Linde K et al. "Are the clinical effects of homoeopathy placebo effects? A meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials." *Lancet* **350**(9081):834–843. Pooled **odds ratio ≈ 2.45 (95% CI 2.05–2.93)** favouring homeopathy over placebo — i.e. the aggregate signal was **not zero.** But the same authors concluded there was **insufficient evidence that homeopathy is clearly efficacious for any single condition**, and in a 1999 follow-up noted the effect was **overestimated by lower-quality trials.**
- **Shang 2005 (skeptical result) [M]:** Shang A et al. "Are the clinical effects of homoeopathy placebo effects? Comparative study of placebo-controlled trials of homoeopathy and allopathy." *Lancet* **366**(9487):726–732. Matched **110 homeopathy trials to 110 conventional trials**; when restricted to **larger, higher-quality** trials, the homeopathy effect was **compatible with placebo** (while conventional medicine's was not). Accompanied by the editorial "The end of homoeopathy."
- **The complication cuts both ways [I]:** Lüdtke & Rutten (2008) showed Shang's conclusion is **sensitive to which subset of "larger higher-quality" trials is chosen** — a publication/selection-bias argument that damages the *strength* of the negative claim even though it does not resurrect a positive one. Publication bias plausibly inflates **both** literatures. Australia's **NHMRC 2015** review concluded no reliable evidence homeopathy is effective for any condition.

### A7. Nobel-laureate endorsement — Brian Josephson [L]
- Brian Josephson (Physics Nobel 1973, the Josephson effect) has **publicly defended** the plausibility of water memory and homeopathy and coined the phrase **"pathological disbelief"** to describe reflexive rejection by scientists (Nobel laureates' meeting talk, 2004; various essays).
- **Grade [L]:** This is **advocacy / expert opinion, not data.** It is genuinely on the record and belongs in the file; it carries no experimental weight on its own.

### A8. 2015–2026 physicochemical work [M as measurements; I/L as interpretation]
- **Elia (Naples group) calorimetry & conductometry [M/I]:** Vittorio Elia and colleagues (University of Naples Federico II) published a long series in *Journal of Thermal Analysis and Calorimetry* and related journals reporting that **"extremely diluted solutions" (EDS)** prepared by iterative dilution + succussion show **measurable, dilution-dependent anomalies**: excess heat of mixing with acid/base, altered electrical conductivity, and density changes — effects that **grow with the number of succussion steps** and persist over time. The **measurements are real bench calorimetry [M]**; the interpretation that they evidence a persistent "structure" carrying information is **contested [I]** (candidate mundane causes: leached silica/nanoparticles from glass, dissolved gases, CO₂). See also Elia et al. on "aqueous nanostructures."
- **Jerman / BION (Slovenia) [L]:** Igor Jerman and collaborators report EM/biophoton and ultra-weak-luminescence differences in treated water; not independently replicated.
- **Cartwright — solvatochromic-dye / impedance studies (2016+) [M/I]:** Steven Cartwright reported that **solvatochromic dyes** (whose color responds to their electronic environment) show reproducible shifts in serially diluted/succussed preparations, offering a *quantitative* probe; measurements are documented [M], mechanism contested [I].
- **Floating water bridge [M]:** A free-standing water bridge forms between two beakers under high voltage (Fuchs et al., 2007 onward) — a **genuine, reproducible phenomenon** showing water can hold unusual macroscopic structure under a field. It is **real physics [M]** but is **not evidence of "memory"**; it requires the sustaining field.

## PART B — SKEPTICAL RECORD

### B1. The Nature-supervised replication, July 1988 [M]
- **Citation:** Maddox J, Randi J, Stewart WW. "'High-dilution' experiments a delusion." *Nature* **334**(6180):287–290, 28 July 1988.
- **Blow by blow [M]:** One week after publication, editor **John Maddox**, magician/skeptic **James Randi**, and NIH fraud investigator **Walter Stewart** visited Benveniste's INSERM lab.
  1. Early **open (unblinded)** runs reproduced the dilution-peak effect — the team watched it "work."
  2. The team then imposed **strict blinding**: sample identities were **coded, the codes sealed in foil and taped to the ceiling** of the lab, out of reach, so no one present knew which tubes were active vs. control during counting.
  3. Under blinding the **peaks disappeared**; results were **consistent with chance.**
- **Conclusion [M]:** The investigators attributed the original result to **unblinded observer bias** in reading basophil counts, plus selective data handling. "The claims are not to be believed."

### B2. Benveniste's specific objections to the procedure [M as objections; I as merit]
- Benveniste **refused to retract** (rebuttal, *Nature* **334**:291). His objections, on record:
  - The visiting team's protocol **departed from his own** (sampling, counting, timing).
  - The **atmosphere was hostile/theatrical** (a magician "policing" a biology lab); he likened it to **"Salem witch-hunts"** and McCarthyism.
  - Blinding introduced **handling changes** he said could kill a genuine but fragile effect.
- These objections are the crux of the **"replication conditions dispute"** — genuinely unresolved as a matter of logic: a null result under changed conditions cannot *by itself* distinguish "no effect" from "effect destroyed by the change."

### B3. Independent replication attempts that failed [M]
- **Hirst et al. 1993 [M]:** Hirst SJ, Hayes NA, Burridge J, Pearce FL, Foreman JC. "Human basophil degranulation is not triggered by very dilute antiserum against human IgE." *Nature* **366**(6455):525–527. Followed Benveniste's method closely across a large blinded run; **"no evidence for any periodic or polynomial change of degranulation as a function of anti-IgE dilution."**
- **Ovelgönne et al. 1992 [M]:** Reproduction attempt of the basophil work — failed to find the high-dilution effect.
- **BBC *Horizon* / Randi $1M challenge, 2002–2003 [M]:** "Homeopathy: The Test." Building on Madeleine Ennis's (Queen's University Belfast) reports of basophil effects at high dilution (Ennis, *Inflammation Research*, 2004), a **blinded protocol run for the programme (Wellcome/UCL involvement) failed** to reproduce the effect; the **$1,000,000 James Randi prize went unclaimed.** (Ennis's own positive reports are logged in A-side literature as [L] — not reproduced under blinding.)
- **Jonas / Ives multi-site blinded test (2004–2006) [M]:** A US team (incl. Wayne Jonas) ran a blinded protocol of the Benveniste/digital-biology effect and **did not confirm** it.

### B4. Physical-chemistry consensus: water has no long-lived memory [M / I]
- **Cowan et al. 2005 [M]:** Cowan ML, Bruner BD, Huse N, Dwyer JR, Chugh B, Nibbering ETJ, Elsaesser T, Miller RJD. "Ultrafast memory loss and energy redistribution in the hydrogen bond network of liquid H₂O." *Nature* **434**(7030):199–202. Femtosecond 2D-IR spectroscopy shows the hydrogen-bond network **loses structural memory within ≈ 50 femtoseconds**, with individual H-bonds making/breaking on a **~1 picosecond** timescale.
- **Interpretation [I]:** The mainstream inference is that **no molecular "imprint" can survive** beyond picoseconds, so a stored "memory" of a solute over the minutes/days homeopathy requires is physically excluded **by the known dynamics of the network.** This is the strongest skeptical physics argument. Its one honest limit: it rules out memory carried by *individual H-bond configurations*; proponents relocate the claim to *collective* structures (domains, nanobubbles, nanoparticles), which femtosecond H-bond data do not directly address.
- **Chemistry-consensus critique [I]:** By Avogadro accounting, dilutions beyond ~10⁻²³ contain **no molecule of solute**; any effect must come from the **solvent/vessel**, not the nominal remedy. Proposed physical carriers (silica nanoparticles, dissolved gases/nanobubbles) are **mundane and do not encode specificity**, which is the property a "remedy" needs.

## PART C — WHAT COMPLICATES *BOTH* SIDES (held honestly)

1. **The replication-conditions dispute (cuts for A and against B).** The 1988 and later nulls were obtained under protocols Benveniste disputed. A changed-condition null cannot logically separate "no effect" from "real-but-fragile effect destroyed by the change." This weakens the *strength* (not the *fact*) of the negative result.
2. **Montagnier never retracted, never independently replicated (cuts both ways).** Absence of retraction is **not** validation; absence of neutral replication is **not** refutation. The correct status is **"open and unexamined by a disinterested lab,"** which is uncomfortable for confident claims in either direction.
3. **Pollack's data are real; the interpretation is not settled (cuts for A on data, against A on theory).** Skeptics who wave away the EZ entirely are wrong about the measurements; proponents who call it a proven "fourth phase" are ahead of the evidence.
4. **Publication bias cuts both directions.** It plausibly inflates positive homeopathy trials **and** weakens Shang-style negatives that depend on trial-subset selection (Lüdtke & Rutten 2008).
5. **Energy/attention asymmetry.** Almost all A-side positive results come from a **small number of committed labs** (Benveniste lineage, Naples, Pollack); almost all replications are by **skeptics.** Neither configuration is neutral.

## PART D — OPEN QUESTIONS (specific, unresolved on the record)

1. Does a **specific, dilution-dependent** basophil/enzyme effect survive a **fully blinded, pre-registered, multi-lab** protocol *co-designed* by both a proponent lab and a skeptic lab (removing the "changed conditions" objection)? Never cleanly done.
2. Are Elia's calorimetric anomalies caused by **leached silica/nanoparticles or dissolved gas**, or by something those controls cannot explain? Decisive control experiments (plastic vs. glass vessels, degassed vs. gassed, filtered nanoparticles) exist but have not been run to consensus.
3. Is Pollack's EZ a **long-range ordered phase** or the sum of **interfacial ion-exchange + diffusiophoresis + double-layer** effects? A quantitative model contest against the *same* datasets is unresolved.
4. Can **any** neutral lab reproduce Montagnier's EM-signal-from-DNA measurement, with instrumentation blinded to sample identity? Not attempted by a disinterested group.
5. Do QED coherence domains make **any falsifiable prediction** distinguishable from ordinary water? Until they do, A5 stays [L].

## PART E — WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT (concrete tests, cost where estimable)

1. **Adversarial-collaboration basophil trial.** Proponent lab + skeptic lab jointly write a **pre-registered, blinded** protocol; samples coded by a third party; analysis plan locked; run at ≥3 sites. Removes the 1988 objection permanently. *Est. $300k–$800k*; the single highest-value test in the whole file.
2. **Elia controls, done to closure.** Repeat the calorimetry/conductometry in **inert plastic vs. borosilicate vs. quartz**, **degassed vs. saturated**, with **nanoparticle-tracking analysis (NTA)** on every sample. If anomalies vanish with plastic + degassing + filtration → mundane; if they persist → genuinely open. *Est. $50k–$150k.*
3. **EZ mechanism bake-off.** Publish the raw EZ datasets; invite conventional-transport modelers and Pollack's group to fit the **same data**. Add isotope/ion-substitution and Nafion-vs-inert-surface controls. Cheap, decisive on interpretation. *Est. <$100k.*
4. **Blinded Montagnier replication.** A neutral EM/biophysics lab reproduces the signal-detection apparatus with sample identity blinded and a sham-signal arm. *Est. $150k–$400k.* Either result advances the record.
5. **Falsifiable QED-domain prediction.** Del Giudice-school theorists specify one measurable that ordinary-water physics forbids (e.g. a specific coherent-emission line under defined excitation), then a spectroscopy lab looks for it. Cost = an experiment, not a program.

*No verdict is entered. The record above is the deliverable.*
