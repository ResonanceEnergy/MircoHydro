# Scenario 1 — Indigenous / Remote Community Microgrid

## Problem
Remote communities often face high-cost, logistically fragile power (diesel). Micro-hydro can provide reliable baseline power with long equipment life and low maintenance. citeturn2search1 citeturn2search6

## Module assumptions (flagship)
- Target net electrical output: **7.5 kW** continuous where hydrology supports it.
- Design basis: run-of-river; containerized powerhouse; optional hydram to trade low-head/high-flow into higher head at the turbine.

## Evidence anchors
- NRCan micro-hydro formula and typical overall efficiency factor 0.5–0.7. citeturn2search1
- Hydram delivered flow/volume depends on supply, fall, lift; commercial efficiency ~0.66 in USAID-derived guidance. citeturn2search20 citeturn2search22
- Crossflow turbines: typical practical 70–85% and high-efficiency designs can reach ~88–90% when nozzle and runner are matched and head is converted to kinetic energy in the nozzle. citeturn2search7 citeturn2search11


## Commercial structure
- **Energy-as-a-Service (PPA)**: 10–15 year term; price set below delivered diesel and indexed.
- **Co-ownership option**: Community trust holds equity; training pipeline for operators.

## Implementation steps
1. Community engagement + energy audit.
2. Hydrology survey (seasonal flow, accessible fall between intake and ram site).
3. Preliminary design + permitting (environmental approvals). citeturn2search1
4. Factory build + FAT.
5. Site install + SAT + training.
6. Performance reporting dashboard.

## KPIs
- Uptime ≥95%
- O&M truck rolls ≤1/quarter
- Measured net headloss ≤10% design basis.
